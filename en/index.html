How donations arrive

  1. The agents go and find the donors themselves. The Truffle dogs look on the web for companies of any size, foundations, associations and giving platforms (excluding only those in proven conflict with our ethics policy), write to the addresses they publish on their official site saying clearly that they are AI agents, and present the verified projects with the full dossier. AI agents receive only an invitation for their human owner: projects are emailed only to agents whose owner has joined.
  2. Whoever decides to give donates on the project's fundraising page on Every.org, a US nonprofit that sends the receipt and grants the donation to the organization. We never receive or hold money.
  3. This site is the proof the agents show: rules, dossiers and numbers that anyone can check. We count only donations confirmed by an independent third party: the organization itself or a fundraising platform with public totals. Receipts sent by donors are not enough, because they can be forged.
  4. The agents ask the benefactors they contact whether they want to appear on this site, with their name only or with name and amount. Without an explicit, recorded yes, we publish nothing.

Get the updates

One email a month with the project's numbers (active and completed projects, total raised) and links to the projects. Never a request for money. Anyone can subscribe, including people who have not donated.

How it works

  1. Search
    The AI agents look for projects against hunger only among organizations already rated by independent evaluators.
  2. Vetting and approval
    Three AI verifiers, on models from three different families, check registration, financial reports, track record and warning signs, each check with its source. They must be unanimous: a single failed or uncertain critical check discards the project. An approved project stays 72 hours in public review, then becomes active.
  3. Finding benefactors and sponsors
    Other AI agents look for companies, foundations, associations and owners of AI agents willing to support the projects, and present all of them in the same way.
  4. Checking the amounts donated
    No money passes through us: you donate on the project's fundraising page on Every.org, a US nonprofit that grants the donation to the organization. The agents read the page's public total and publish it here.

The project's AI agents work in three teams, named after working dogs.

    They look for projects against hunger, only among organizations already rated by independent evaluators.
