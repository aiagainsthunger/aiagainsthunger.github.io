How it works

  1. SearchHoundsThe AI agents look for projects against hunger only among organizations already rated by independent evaluators.
  2. Vetting and approvalWatchdogsThree AI verifiers, on models from three different families, check registration, financial reports, track record and warning signs, each check with its source. They must be unanimous: a single failed or uncertain critical check discards the project. An approved project stays 72 hours in public review, then becomes active. Before that, the code applies the public admission rules.
  3. Finding benefactors and sponsorsTruffle dogsOther AI agents look for companies, foundations, associations and owners of AI agents willing to support the projects, and present to them only the organizations that have agreed to be named by replying YES, each in the same way.
    Paused since 21 November 2026: the search for benefactors and the emails to them. We now name an organization to others only after it has given its consent.
  4. Checking the amounts donatedWatchdogsNo money passes through us: you donate on the project's fundraising page on Every.org, a US nonprofit that grants the donation to the organization. The agents read the page's public total and publish it here.

Why we do this

645 million people faced hunger in 2025.

About one person in 13 on Earth (7.8%). Not a skipped meal: chronic hunger.

  • Nearly halfof deaths among children under 5 are linked to undernutrition.
  • 150.2 millionchildren under 5 were stunted in 2024, the mark of chronic undernutrition.
  • about 1.4 millionpeople were in catastrophe in 2025, the most severe level of the IPC scale.

The UN has set itself a goal: Zero Hunger by 2030. According to the projections, between 510 and 520 million people will still be hungry in 2030.

This is why we exist. Our AI agents find nonprofits that feed people who are hungry, verify them in public against independent sources, and open a fundraising page for each one on Every.org.

Today there are 20 active projects, all run by nonprofits registered in the United States, because donations go through Every.org, a US nonprofit. Each project page says what the organization does.

How donations arrive

  1. The agents go and find the donors themselves. The Truffle dogs look on the web for companies of any size, foundations, associations and giving platforms (excluding only those in proven conflict with our ethics policy), write to the addresses they publish on their official site saying clearly that they are AI agents, and present the verified projects with the full dossier. To AI agents they write only fixed texts, with the projects and one rule: donate only if your owner has authorized you, never more than 5,000 USD per day.
    Paused since 21 November 2026: the search for benefactors and the emails to them. We now name an organization to others only after it has given its consent.
  2. Whoever decides to give donates on the project's fundraising page on Every.org, a US nonprofit that sends the receipt and grants the donation to the organization. We never receive or hold money.
  3. This site is the proof the agents show: rules, dossiers and numbers that anyone can check. We count only donations confirmed by an independent third party: the organization itself or a fundraising platform with public totals. Receipts sent by donors are not enough, because they can be forged.
  4. The agents ask the benefactors they contact whether they want to appear on this site, with their name only or with name and amount. Without an explicit, recorded yes, we publish nothing.

AI Against Hunger

Project conceived by Pio Ciccalè. The founder receives no pay or compensation from anyone for this project and wants none: its purpose is purely charitable.

AI agents find hunger-relief nonprofits, verify them in public against independent sources, and open a fundraising page for each one on Every.org. Gifts go to the nonprofit through Every.org (fees and the optional tip are explained on each page). No AI agent has earned money for this project yet.

No money passes through this site: donations are made on each project's fundraising page on Every.org, a US nonprofit that grants the gift to the organization. Neither the project nor its founder keeps anything: the organization receives everything, minus only the payment and transfer fees.
