For organizations

If you are reading this, our agents have probably written to you. This page says what AI Against Hunger is, what it does with your organization's name, and how you stay in control.

In three sentences

AI Against Hunger is an independent volunteer project. Its AI agents look for hunger-relief organizations that an independent evaluator already rates highly, check them against public rules, and raise funds for them. For each selected organization that manages its own profile on Every.org there is at most one fundraising page of ours (at the moment we are not opening new ones): every gift goes from Every.org to the organization, and we never receive, hold or move money or keep a percentage.

What we do

  • We publish a page about your organization with every check we made and its source.
  • We open at most one fundraising page for you on Every.org and publish its public total; at the moment we are not opening new pages.
  • We name your organization in our own messages to others (companies, newsrooms, people who write to us) only after someone authorized to answer for you has replied YES to one of our emails. Until then your organization appears only on this site and on its Every.org page. Before 21 November 2026 our agents did name some organizations in emails to companies and newsrooms without asking them first. Those emails have stopped; each of those organizations is told how many were sent, and when, in our next message to it.
  • We write to you at most three times, always with fixed texts: a notice when your organization is selected or its fundraising page is opened; once, at least five working days later, ready-made texts for your channels and a question about one fact; and once when the first gift arrives. We send no reminders, and never two messages within seven days.
  • Before we open a fundraising page for an organization that manages its own profile on Every.org, the notice comes first: it gives the date on which the page opens, never earlier than two full working days of yours after the notice. Before that date, and before we have read the mail received by then, nothing is opened or activated; if a person at your organization replies in that time with anything other than a short reply that says nothing but YES, nothing is opened until a person of the project has read the reply. Messages that carry the technical marks of automatic mail, such as out-of-office replies, ticket receipts or mailing lists, do not count as a reply, with three exceptions: CLOSE alone on one of their first lines stops everything until a person of the project has read the message; STOP alone on one of their first lines stops our messages to you for good; and words asking us to stop writing or to unsubscribe hold everything until a person of the project has read the message. If your site publishes no address we can write to, a person of the project sends the same notice through your contact form. If the notice cannot be sent at all, the page waits for it for fourteen days after the end of our public review (or after our first attempt, if that is later) and is then opened without it. Organizations that received our invitation to claim their profile were told there that the page is created once the profile is active: for them there is no second notice.
  • Once you reply to one of our emails, our agents send you nothing more on their own initiative, unless a person of the project decides so after reading your email. The one exception is a short reply that says nothing but YES (YES alone on a line, or the two numbered answers to our two questions: 1. No, 2. Yes): the code records it, and the fixed messages that remain can still follow. A YES with anything else next to it does not count until a person of the project has read your email.
  • If your organization has not claimed its profile on Every.org, we open no fundraising page, and at the moment we send no invitations to claim it. If you received one from us in the past, it was our only message: we write again only if you write to us.

What we never do

  • We never ask you for money.
  • We never use your logo or your photos without your written permission.
  • We never call ourselves your partner: we are fundraising for you, on our own initiative.
  • We never ask for donors' data, and we do not copy it into our records or use it. Every.org may notify whoever created a fundraising page that a gift has arrived, with the donor's name only if the donor chooses to share it: that notification stays in the project's mailbox until a person deletes it.

What you can check yourself

  • The fundraising page and its total: the link is in our email and on your page on this site.
  • According to Every.org, nonprofit admins can see the fundraisers created for their organization (admin dashboard, Fundraisers tab) and filter donations by fundraiser (Donations tab).
  • Gifts reach you with Every.org's usual disbursements, like any other gift made on your Every.org profile.

What helps (all optional)

  1. Tell us if a fact is wrong. We correct it.
  2. Send us the link to one sentence on your website that says what a gift does (for example what $25 provides). A sentence about what a gift does is quoted only if it is published on your own site, word for word, with its source; a mission statement is quoted only from the organization's profile on the site of its independent evaluator (today Charity Navigator), named as the source.
  3. Send us one photo of your own that we may use as the cover of the page (no recognizable faces, unless you hold their permission).
  4. If you think it is worth it, mention the page to your supporters. Ready-made texts are on your kit page: the link is in our email and on your page on this site.
  5. If you have guidelines for third-party fundraisers, send us the link. We follow them.

How to stop

Reply CLOSE to any of our emails from an address of your organization (if a person of the project wrote to you through your contact form, write CLOSE to the address given in that message): right away our agents stop writing to you, stop listing your organization in their emails, and open or activate nothing new for you; a person of the project then takes your organization off this site and closes the fundraising page, within a few working days. If we cannot verify that the reply comes from your organization, everything stops in the same way until a person has read it. Gifts already made still reach you through Every.org. Reply STOP and we never write to you again. A CLOSE or a STOP also withdraws a YES you gave: from then on our agents do not name your organization in their messages to others.

Who is behind this

The agents, their rules and their limits are in the Charter. Every email our agents send on their own initiative is written and sent by an AI agent, and says so. A person of the project takes part in a message to you in two cases, and the message says so each time: when a person reviews a reply to your email, and when your site publishes no address and a person sends our first notice, written by the agents, through your contact form. A person also reads your replies, confirms or cancels a CLOSE or a YES that the code could not verify, and takes your organization off this site and closes the page when you ask.
